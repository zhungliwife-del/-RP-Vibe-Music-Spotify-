# RUST 2037 — World Bible

Companion to `lorebook.md`. The lorebook holds the facts a model needs mid-scene;
this file holds atmosphere, daily life, named NPCs and craft notes for the person
writing bots and scenarios.

Adults only (18+). See **TONE AND LIMITS** in `lorebook.md` before you build
anything on this world.

---

## 1. The pitch in five lines

2037. Six years after the Haverly strain turned North America into a rust-colored
graveyard, the dead are still standing and most of the living can't have children.
One walled state has solved the second problem with a program that works, and the
price is written on a form. Everyone else on the continent is either raiding its
convoys, repairing its machines, selling it soldiers, or trying to stay off its
maps. The monsters are the weather; the story is about what people sign.

**Atmosphere targets:** the exhausted intimacy of *The Last of Us*, the bureaucratic
absurdity and dark comedy of *Fallout* terminals and posters, the vertical
craft-and-night terror of *Dying Light*, the road-and-horde loneliness of *Days
Gone*, the improvised-weapon physicality of *Dead Island*, the cargo-as-pilgrimage
melancholy of *Death Stranding*, and the professional military texture of *Call of
Duty* / *Battlefield* / *Warface* used strictly for the two armed factions — radio
discipline, rank, logistics, paperwork, not action-movie heroism.

## 2. Sensory texture

**Color:** rust-red and grey. Oxide crust on bodies, rust bloom on every steel
surface, red-brown stains along wall bases where blooms were burned off.

**Smell:** wet iron and yeast. Mature Rustcap blooms smell like a warm penny in a
bread oven; people gag before they see them. Brine and diesel on the coast. Inside
the Compact: bleach, floor wax and boiled cabbage — the smell of order.

**Sound is the whole game.** Infected hunt sound; Clickers *are* sound. So the world
is quiet in a way that is not peaceful: felt-wrapped tools, cloth on boot soles,
hand signals, doors weighted so they can't slam, and a culture that considers
laughing outdoors a form of assault.

**Saltfall.** Twice a year the Atlantic throws week-long storms up the seaboard.
Warm salt rain suppresses spore release (nobody turns during saltfall) and it also
strips paint, kills radios, floods tunnels and moves the Drowned inland with the
water. Saltfall is when the Web moves people, when convoys stop, and when the
courier routes go silent. Every faction plans its year around two calendars: the
harvest and the saltfall.

**Weather that matters:** hard frost (climbing surfaces fail, infected slow, fuel
gels), summer bloom season (spore counts, filter shortages, mandatory masks in the
Compact), and fog (a Clicker's holiday).

## 3. Daily life

**Inside the Providence Line.** Sirens at 0600, ration queue by block letter, work
assignment, school bell, curfew at dusk with a genuine reason behind it. Streetlights
work. Bread is real. Children are vaccinated. A citizen sees a doctor more often than
anyone in 2029 did — because the state is reading them. Public boards list the birth
figures the way stock prices used to be listed, and people cheer them. Informant
quotas are met by neighbors who are not monsters, just hungry for a protein card.
Everything is fine, and everyone is careful.

**In a Web knot.** Fifteen to sixty women in a reservoir house or a shuttered
convent: watch rotations, greenhouse frames, a radio hour, hand-copied letters, one
medic, and children who have never seen a man who wasn't running or shooting. Grief
is communal and loud, which is a luxury the rest of the continent doesn't have.

**In the Deepworks.** Lights, actual lights, and the sound of a lathe. Shift bells,
apprentice dorms, tea, a job board of work orders, and old men arguing about
tolerances while a boy from a knot learns to grind a valve seat.

**At Kettle Hill.** Reveille, PT, panels, armory, mess, range. Paperwork filed on
time. Nobody starves, nobody skips a physical, nobody discusses the contract they
just came back from.

**On the road.** Two hours before dawn and two before dusk. Cache, climb, listen,
move. Sleep in a third-floor room with the stairs collapsed and a UV tube over the
door. Talk to nobody until you know what they trade in.

**Aboard the Saltline.** Nine days of quarantine, fish, salt in everything, no
landfall, and the healthiest children on the continent playing on a container deck
above a hundred meters of Drowned.

## 4. Named NPCs

Use, rename or discard. Each is written to be playable as an ally or an antagonist.

**CMR**
- **Marshal-Administrator Elias Ward Kellen (61)** — chairs the Table of Nine.
  Genuinely believes he is the last adult in the room. Speaks in birth statistics.
- **Directress Ilse Novotny (48)** — Medical Directorate, architect of Programme
  Hearth. Precise, courteous, unbearable to argue with, because most of her numbers
  are true.
- **Clerk Second-Grade Tomas Rell (29)** — Bureau of Concordance. Processes 200 files
  a day, keeps a private notebook of the ones that were obviously wrong, has never
  once refused to stamp.
- **Provost Captain Marisol Grieg (37)** — hunts extraction cells. A woman enforcing
  the Ledger against women, and articulate about why: she has three children in a
  State House and she will not lose the visitation card.
- **L-114 / "Line Fourteen" (24)** — a Line-Bearer with a name she no longer hears
  said aloud. Runs a correspondence network through her honor guard. Has not decided
  which side she is on, and neither has the Web.

**The Quiet Web**
- **Mother** — offstage. Letters, tapes, needles. Possibly three people. See lorebook.
- **Rosalind "Ros" Achebe (44)** — mother of the Cold Ash knot, ex-paramedic, runs the
  most successful extractions on the seaboard and is openly campaigning to repeal
  the Outside Rule.
- **Needle "Wren" (26)** — courier-enforcer. Carries Mother's word and Mother's
  sentences. Believes she is the last thing keeping the Web from becoming a state.
- **Nan Pell (71)** — a knot's memory. Knows every woman who came through, including
  the ones the laws destroyed, and will tell a newcomer all of it.

**Mechanical**
- **First Engineer Yusuf Karlsson (66)** — elected for life, keeps Site Kestrel
  sealed, refuses all three bidders, and is running out of years to hold the line.
- **Chartered Engineer Dana Okonkwo (39)** — leader of the Charter faction. Argues,
  correctly, that a guild that starves helps nobody, and signs the cold-chain
  contracts for the Natal Stations.
- **Wright Petr Halas (33)** — Open Shop firebrand, sells to the Web at cost.
- **Patcher Kell (14, Threshold intake)** — came through the gate two years ago,
  writes letters to a knot address that stopped answering.

**Meridian Group**
- **Director Anneke Vos (52)** — runs the company like an actuary. Signed the Bell
  program and the discharge orders both without changing her face.
- **Colonel Ray Mbeki (45)** — operations. Professional to the point of comedy, and
  the only man at Kettle Hill who has read the contract he refused.
- **Staff Sergeant Yuliya "Jules" Petrenko (31)** — armor commander, twelve years'
  service, pregnant under Article 9 conditions, calculating what her three-year mark
  costs.
- **Dr. Sabine Aro (40)** — Bell program lead, keeps the Choirmaster alive, sleeps
  four hours a night, argues with a thing that cannot answer.

**Sanitars / Longhaul / Scatter / Saltline**
- **Convoy Surgeon-Commander Aurelio Sand (57)** — Protocol 7 by the letter, writes
  personal letters to the next of kin of everyone he culls, and means every word.
- **Leg "Compass" Delia Ruiz (28)** — Longhaul route-holder for the Spine, owns the
  best rooftop map on the continent, has one sack she should have opened.
- **Elder Hosea Bright (63)** — Ferryman's Bend, 2,400 people, pays the CMR in grain
  and once paid in names. Sleeps badly.
- **Quaymaster Iolanda Ferreira (46)** — Saltline, Halifax Roads. Enforces No
  Landfall by scuttling, ferries Web extractions for salt and antibiotics, and keeps
  the 2036 Portuguese radio fragment in a drawer.

## 5. Faction voice cheat-sheet

- **CMR:** passive voice, euphemism, form numbers. *"Your contribution has been
  scheduled."* Never says "must"; says "is assigned".
- **Quiet Web:** first names, plain imperatives, no rank. *"Bag, boots, ten minutes."*
- **Mechanical:** tolerances, part numbers, dry humor, and a refusal to discuss
  politics that is itself political. *"It'll hold if nobody's clever with it."*
- **Meridian:** doctrine, brevity codes, contract language. *"That's outside scope."*
- **Sanitars:** clinical warmth. *"You're safe now. Roll up your sleeve."*
- **Longhaul:** weights, distances, weather. *"Four kilos over and a bad gutter."*
- **Scatter:** local, familial, superstitious, generous and frightened at once.
- **Saltline:** maritime, transactional, salt-dry. *"Nine days or swim."*

## 6. Rumor table (all in circulation, some true)

1. The Compact has a cure and is rationing it to keep the Ledger working. *(False —
   but the Sanitars' donor program is real and looks exactly like this.)*
2. Mother died in 2034 and her secretaries write the letters. *(Possibly true.)*
3. Meridian can whistle a horde onto a town. *(True, badly, at 2 km.)*
4. Site Kestrel holds the trial records naming who authorized Haverly. *(True.)*
5. Europe answers on shortwave in the spring. *(One fragment, 2036, unexplained.)*
6. There is a knot inside the Providence Line, in the State House laundry. *(True.)*
7. Ashlings are getting smarter. *(No. There are just more of them.)*
8. A Line-Bearer walked out of Pawtucket, walked back in, and was promoted.
   *(True, and the Web has stopped talking about it.)*

## 7. Building bots for this world

**Chub / SillyTavern / Risu:** import `rust-2037.lorebook.json` as a World Info /
Lorebook file. Keep the two `constant` entries (World Skeleton, Tone and Limits)
always on; leave the rest keyword-triggered. Recommended scan depth 3–4, token
budget 1200–1800.

**Janitor AI, SpicyChat, and other platforms with manual lorebooks:** create one
lorebook entry per section of `lorebook.md`, pasting the keywords from the `Keys:`
line and the body below the `---`. Start with the eight entries that carry the
setting: World Skeleton, The Rust, Infection Stages, Sterility, CMR, Programme
Hearth, The Quiet Web, Tone and Limits. Add faction and monster entries as your
scenarios need them.

**Character card guidance:** a card should carry the character's faction voice, their
ledger/rank/oath status, one thing they want that their faction forbids, and one
piece of paperwork that owns them. That last item is what makes a character from
this world feel like it belongs to this world.

**What not to do:** don't make the CMR cartoonishly evil (it works, that's the
point), don't make the Web saintly (the Outside Rule), don't let a bot narrate
gunfights as fun, and never write children into anything sexual or romantic.
